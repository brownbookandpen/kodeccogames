So You Drew an Encounter — Combat, Sabotage & The Math of Survival

Aug 23, 2026 · 3 min read

The Moment the Card Flips

You’ve finished your pre-draw actions: your character ability is on cooldown, you deposited a spare tool into the Shared Inventory, and nobody revealed themselves as a Carrier.

You reach for the central deck, flip the top card, and the room goes quiet.

It’s an Encounter.

Whether it’s a standard Undead Horde (5 DMG), a hulking Behemoth (7 DMG), or The Matriarch (8 DMG), the table suddenly shifts from resource planning into immediate crisis management.

Here's exactly how an Encounter resolves under the new v4.0 rules.

Step 1: Assess the Threat & Check the Danger Gauge

Every Encounter card displays a Target Damage Threshold representing the horde’s strength.

Taking the Undead Horde as our example, the card shows a clear requirement: 5 DAMAGE TO CLEAR.

Step 2: The Leader's Call — Fight or Run?

As the active player (the Turn Leader), the call is yours: fight, or run.

### Option A: Call a "RUN" (Flee the Encounter)

If the table is caught off-guard with empty hands or weak weapons, you can choose to Run (up to 3 Runaways allowed per game).

  • The Immediate Effect: The Undead Horde is temporarily bypassed and parked for later.
  • The Cost (Next Encounter Penalty): As shown on the card (RUN: [+1] DANGER), fleeing adds +1 Danger to the NEXT Zombie Encounter your team faces.

Example: If you choose to run from this Undead Horde, the very next zombie threat drawn will have its target damage increased by +1. Running buys you breathing room now, but makes the very next fight harder to survive.

(Tip: You can actually reclaim spent Run attempts later in the game — more details on this in the Additional Note below.)

Keep in mind: the group is allowed a maximum of 3 Run attempts in total across the entire game. Once all 3 slots are filled, you can no longer flee and must stand and fight.

(Note: Running is distinct from a Failure — fleeing temporarily evades the fight at the cost of Danger, whereas failing occurs when your strike team enters combat and falls short of the damage threshold.)

### Option B: Stand & Assemble the Strike Team

If the Leader decides the group is ready to confront the Undead Horde, you forgo running and commit to combat.

Dynamic Team Sizing (No Fixed Headcounts)

Unlike older versions that forced an artificial player count (e.g., "Choose exactly 4 players"), party size is completely flexible. The Leader chooses who joins the fight based on what weapons players claim to hold:

  • Armed with heavy Tier 2 or Tier 3 weapons? You can send a lean 2-person strike team to conserve team resources.
  • Carrying basic Tier 1 weapons? You’ll need to rally more players to combine enough attacks to hit the 5 Damage threshold.

### The [OVERENCUMBERED] Restriction

Only active, agile survivors can fight. Any player holding 4 or more cards in hand is marked as [OVERENCUMBERED] and cannot be chosen to participate in the encounter until they reduce their hand size to 3 cards or fewer.

### The Social Deduction Dilemma

Before locking in the squad, the Leader relies on verbal table talk. Everyone will claim they can contribute damage — but every player you invite into the strike team is another opportunity for a hidden Carrier to infiltrate the fight and sabotage the outcome.

Contesting the Strike Team (The Group Veto)

Once the Leader nominates the players for the fight, the rest of the table gets a say. If the group distrusts the Leader's selection or suspects that a hidden Carrier was placed on the team, the group can choose to CONTEST the encounter.

What happens when an encounter is Contested:

  • The nomination is immediately cancelled and automatically counts as a RUN.
  • The encounter card is placed face-up on the table as a parked threat.
  • This consumes 1 of the group's 3 maximum Run attempts.
  • The standard Run penalty applies: +1 Danger is added to the next Zombie Encounter.

Contesting gives the survivors a vital emergency brake if they suspect the Leader is setting up a sabotaged team — but it costs a precious Run attempt and raises the danger for the next fight.

Step 3: Submitting Combat Cards (Face-Down & Sabotage)

Once the Leader locks in the strike team, each participating player secretly chooses 1 card from their hand and places it face-down into a central combat pile.

Here is what can be contributed:

Card Type Effect Deck Count
Tier 1 Weapons +1 Attack Damage 20 cards
Tier 2 Weapons +2 Attack Damage 10 cards
Tier 3 Weapons +3 Attack Damage 5 cards
Live Samples (Sabotage) -3 Attack Damage 15 cards

### The -3 Sabotage Dilemma

  • Survivors want to contribute their best weapons to reach the 5 Damage needed to clear the Undead Horde.
  • Carriers want the encounter to fail. By submitting a Sample, they inflict a massive -3 DMG penalty on the group's total attack score.

Step 4: Shuffle, Reveal & Calculate Damage

To protect players' hidden identities and maintain social deduction tension, all submitted combat cards are gathered together, shuffled thoroughly, and then flipped face-up in the center of the table.

Total Team Damage = (Sum of Weapon Damage) − (Sum of Sample Sabotages)

### Scenario A: A Clean Victory (As Shown in Photo)
Revealed Cards Armaments (+1), Armaments (+1), Medical (+2), Armaments (+1)
Calculation 1 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 5 Total Damage
Result VICTORY (5 is equal to or greater than 5). The horde is defeated with zero sabotage.

The reveal: Armaments (+1), Armaments (+1), Medical (+2), and Armaments (+1).

With zero sabotage cards played, the strike team successfully hits the 5 Damage threshold needed to clear the Undead Horde!

### Scenario B: The Carrier Sabotage

Let's say one player secretly dropped a Sample instead of a weapon:
Revealed Cards Armaments (+1), Medical (+2), Medical (+2), Sample (-3)
Calculation 1 + 2 + 2 − 3 = 2 Total Damage
Result DEFEAT (2 is less than 5). The fight crashes because a hidden Carrier successfully sabotaged the squad.

Because a hidden Carrier secretly contributed a Sample (-3 DMG Sabotage) instead of a weapon, the team only dealt 2 damage out of the 5 required to clear the Undead Horde. The encounter fails, the group suffers the [+1] Mutation penalty, and the table immediately begins interrogating the 4 strike team members to figure out who dropped the sabotage.

Step 5: Outcome — Victory vs. Defeat

Once the damage is tallied against the Undead Horde's 5 Damage threshold, resolve the encounter based on the total.

### On Victory (Total Damage is 5 or higher)

  • The Undead Horde is defeated and placed into the discard pile.
  • All 4 submitted combat cards go to the discard pile (ready for Rangers like the Forager or Blacksmith to scavenge later during Daybreak).
  • Participating character passives activate (such as the Survivalist's RECOVER ability granting a random Supply card refund).
  • Any active Danger modifier on this fight is cleared.

### On Defeat (Total Damage is less than 5)

  • The group suffers the penalty listed on the card: FAILURE: [+1] MUTATION. Advance the mutation tracker by 1.
  • Any active Danger penalties remain in effect.
  • The Interrogation Phase Begins: The table now has concrete proof that a Carrier was on the strike team and secretly dropped the -3 Sample sabotage. Accusations, bluffs, and deductions start immediately as players try to figure out who lied about their hand!

Additional Note: Reclaiming Run Attempts

Encounters the group ran from earlier don't have to stay parked forever. Once you've scavenged better weapons, you can choose to go back and clear them.

On any turn, a player may skip their normal draw entirely and instead challenge one of the face-up encounters they bypassed earlier.

If the strike team successfully defeats that parked encounter:

  1. The encounter card is cleared and placed into the discard pile.
  2. The team reduces its accumulated run count, successfully reclaiming 1 spent Run attempt for future emergencies.
  3. Failed Encounters are placed into the discard pile, with their debuff enacted onto the group.

This gives the survivors a meaningful tactical choice: spend a turn to eliminate a known threat and regain an emergency escape slot, or keep pushing deeper into the unknown deck.

What This Brings to the Table

By connecting encounter resolution directly to tiered weapon math, dynamic team sizes, and the ever-present threat of a -3 Sample sabotage, combat in Carrier is no longer just a numbers check. Every encounter becomes a high-stakes interrogation.

As the Leader, you're constantly weighing the odds: risk bringing along a player you don't fully trust because you desperately need their firepower, or head into the horde shorthanded and hope your basic weapons are enough?

We're bringing this updated combat system to our next playtest sessions to see how players handle the pressure. Let us know your thoughts — see you in the next dev log!
