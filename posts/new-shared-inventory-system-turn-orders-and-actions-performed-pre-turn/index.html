New Shared Inventory System, Turn Orders, and Actions Performed Pre-Turn

Aug 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Cleaning Up the Turn Loop

When we stripped down Carrier from 198 cards to 89, we fixed our setup bloat. But streamlining the deck forced us to take a hard look at the actual turn-to-turn mechanics: What happens right before an Encounter is drawn, and how do players manage their gear?

In earlier versions, trading was messy, players hoarded cards indefinitely, and Carriers would shout out revelations in the middle of other players' actions—often triggering arguments when an encounter card was drawn at the exact same split-second.

To fix this, we structured the turn around a strict Pre-Draw Action Window, introduced a Shared Group Inventory, and re-engineered the Carrier elimination timing.

Here is how a turn flows in the new build.

1. Setting the Stage: The First Turn

Once every player receives their Character (Public Role) and their Allegiance (Secret Role), the game is ready to begin.

  • First Player: The youngest player at the table takes the first turn.
  • The Opening Call: The starting player decides whether the group begins the journey in Daybreak or Nightfall, setting the active phase for the entire first rotation around the table.

2. The Pre-Draw Window (Actions Before You Draw)

Before a player touches the Encounter Deck, they enter their Pre-Draw window. Any pre-draw actions must be completed before drawing an encounter card.

On your turn, you may take any of the following actions:

Activate Character Ability: If your character matches the current phase (Daybreak, Nightfall, or All Phase) and you have remaining uses.

Interact with the Shared Inventory: Contribute an item to—or take an item from—the group's central inventory pool.

Contributing to the Shared Inventory is done anonymously.

But when you wish to take from the Shared Inventory, you can go through all the cards and choose the one you want to take.

Carrier Reveal & Elimination: If you are a Carrier, you may choose this moment to reveal your secret role and eliminate a player.

Once these actions are resolved (or passed), the player draws from the Encounter Deck to face the horde.

3. The Shared Group Inventory & The "Overencumbered" Rule

Direct player-to-player card passing caused downtime and clunky negotiations. We replaced private trading with a Central Shared Inventory.

Instead of handing cards directly across the table, players deposit useful items into the common pool or pull gear out to prepare for upcoming fights.

To keep gear moving and prevent players from sitting on massive armories, we introduced a strict hand size rule:

Hand Size Status Encounters
Up to 3 cards Standard Capacity Eligible to fight
4+ cards Overencumbered Cannot fight

Why allow hoarding? The strategy of being Overencumbered

A player can technically hold more than 3 cards, but doing so marks them as Overencumbered.

  • The Restriction: An overencumbered player cannot participate in any Encounters until they reduce their hand size back down to 3 cards or fewer.
  • The Tactical Dilemma (Survivor): Do you temporarily sideline yourself from combat to safely transport multiple high-tier weapons or vital Samples for the cure—or to keep some in reserve for your Character ability, if it requires them?
  • The Tactical Dilemma (Carrier): As a hidden Carrier, do you intentionally hoard items under the guise of "holding resources," quietly removing yourself from fights and forcing the rest of the group to face the horde shorthanded?

4. Fixing the Carrier Misfire: No More Mid-Turn Kills

One of the biggest procedural changes in this build is how and when Carriers can strike.

Previously, Carriers could reveal their secret role at almost any time, including between other players' turns. While exciting in theory, in practice it created constant table friction: a player would reach to draw an encounter, a Carrier would yell to reveal, and the table would get bogged down arguing over which action took priority.

The New Rule: Carriers can no longer reveal between turns. A Carrier must wait until their own turn and reveal during their Pre-Draw action window to eliminate a target.

What this means for balance:

  • Zero Timing Disputes: No more overlapping actions, misfired card draws, or contested priority calls.
  • A Distinct Trade-off: This does give Survivors a slight predictability window—you know you won't be assassinated mid-draw on your own turn. However, it forces Carriers to play far more calculatingly, timing their turn order and using the Pre-Draw window to strike with maximum psychological impact.

5. The New Turn Cycle at a Glance

Step Phase What Happens
1 Start Turn The active player begins their turn.
2 Pre-Draw Actions Choose any/all: use Character Ability (if Phase matches); deposit to/withdraw from the Shared Inventory; reveal as Carrier to eliminate (optional).
3 Check Hand Capacity Over 3 cards = Overencumbered.
4 Draw Encounter Card The player draws from the Encounter Deck.
5 Resolve Encounter The Leader selects the participating party; Overencumbered players cannot fight.
6 End Turn Play passes to the next player.

What's Next?

Restricting the Carrier kill window is a deliberate design choice to prioritize smooth table flow over chaotic interruptions. We'll be watching this closely during upcoming playtests to see if Carriers feel appropriately lethal or if they need compensatory tools.
