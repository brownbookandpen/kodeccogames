Title : Characters Changes (Working Title)

So, mainly this is an update for characters. A lot of the characters used on the site are from Version 3. We've already redesigned the Encounters, the Secret Roles, and overhauled the Samples and Supplies and how those work during the setup phase.

And finally, once all of that was in place, we moved onto Character Changes. Characters had to be last, because it's probably the one part that's most dependent on everything else.

Version 4.0 was basically the characters we brought over from 3.0 that were the best. But with 4.1, we wanted to revamp the existing ones and work on them to make them even better.

Now, let's talk about why we wanted to change things.

# 1. Our characters all kind of looked the same.

![](2.%20Faces%20are%20too%20similar%20and%20not%20unique.png)

As you can see, our female characters have a very similar face structure, and it can seem very low effort, especially if it goes out as the final product.

# 2. Outfits felt too clean for an apocalypse.

![](3.%20Clothes%20and%20style%20feels%20a%20bit%20boring.png)

It's not that we didn't work hard on the original designs. But you get better as you go, and our skill has moved on a fair bit since we drew these. So it just felt like the right time to redo them properly.

Honestly, I think part of why this game has been so hard to publish is that it never really felt finished to us.

The art can always be better. And we don't want to put out something uncompleted, or anything short of the best we can actually do.

# 3. Once you used your ability, that was it for the rest of the game.

![](4.%20Abilities%20can%20only%20be%20used%20once%20per%20game.png)

We wanted to change that completely. Now most, if not all, characters can repeatedly use their abilities. So we can ensure that players are engaged, and you always have something to do during your turn.

If you look at any 4.1 card, you'll notice the middle line is just gone. Every old card had three lines: the phase, the usage limit, then the effect. The new ones only have two. The phase, and the effect.

That's the whole idea in one change. We stopped limiting abilities by a counter, and started limiting them by **cost** and **timing** instead. Some characters now pay a supply card to use their ability. Some are locked to one phase. Some have a drawback baked right into the text. All of those are better limiters than "you get one, good luck", because they're decisions you make during the game instead of a number that runs out.

The other thing you'll notice is the card art itself. We rebuilt the template. There's a proper light source behind the character now instead of a flat forest, the frames are cleaner, and every character got repainted with more detail. Straps, pouches, wear and tear, actual gear. The old cast looked like campers. This one looks like people who've been out there a while.

So, with all these in mind, we're finally ready to release version 4.1. We're going to go through them individually, so it's gonna be quite a long post.

So let's begin.

# GENETICIST [SCIENTIST]

![](%5BA%5D%20GENETICIST.png)

With the Geneticist, we've used the old female model from "Paramedic". If you've playtested a few times you'll know that the Paramedic is an underutilized character whose ability is to heal players who lost their ability, which is quite a limited and dependent ability, because as the Paramedic, if no one loses their ability, you're just waiting for the game to conclude. Not interesting.

Now, the ability of Geneticist is only usable during Daybreak, which was already a limiter that controlled the ability usage for players, so they don't spam it every turn. You have to wait between phases to use it. Feels like that's already a good limiter on its own. So we removed the "can be used once per game" line entirely. The ability text is otherwise untouched. Ask a player to reveal their Secret Role, and if they agree, you reveal yours to them too.

# VIROLOGIST [SCIENTIST]

![](%5BA%5D%20VIROLOGIST.png)

Art wise this one is a lighter touch. Same character, same beanie, but repainted properly. The old jacket had a big white fur collar and was fairly shapeless. The new one is a proper bomber jacket with a unit patch, cleaner shading.

The ability change is the important part. Old Diagnose was "once per turn, **discard 3 cards** to check a player's Secret Role."

New Diagnose: "**Discard 1 [MEDICAL] supply card** to check a player's Secret Role."

Three cards was a brutal cost. It didn't matter *which* three, so it just punished you for having a hand. Now that Supplies are typed, we can charge you something specific instead. One Medical card. That's a smaller cost in raw cards but a sharper one in practice, because Medical is something other people want too, and you have to actually go find it.

This is the pattern you'll see a lot in 4.1. The ability got cheaper to use, but you have to have the right thing on hand to use it.

# OUTLANDER [AMNESIAC]

![](%5BB%5D%20OUTLANDER.png)

The old Outlander was just the bandaged guy in a plain coat, holding a book. The wrappings were the only thing on him doing any work. Everything else about the design was saying nothing at all.

The bandages stay, because that's the character. He's the man with no memory and no face. What changed is everything built around them. Wide brim hat, long coat, waistcoat, cravat, bandaged hands, staff in hand. Now he reads as a drifter who has been walking a very long time, instead of a hospital patient who wandered into the woods. It also gives us a silhouette nobody else in the cast has, which was part of the problem we talked about up top.

The ability is where the real change is. Old Flashback was "twice per game, look at another player's Secret Role", a plain peek with a counter on it.

Flashback: **you may never look at your own Secret Role, but on your turn during Daybreak, you may look at another player's.**

That drawback isn't new. He's an Amnesiac, he was never able to look at his own Secret Role. It's just that in 4.1 we finally put it on the card instead of leaving it to the category box. And now that the twice-per-game limit is gone, that one restriction is carrying the whole card. He can look at somebody new every Daybreak, all game long. He just can never look at himself.

# DUELIST [HUNTER]

![](%5BBLUE%5D%20DUELIST.png)

The old Duelist was the one that stuck out the most stylistically. Twin swords, blue tunic, round glasses. He looked like he walked in from a different game entirely. Nothing about him said apocalypse. Duelist was always a rework of the old Brawler, so this was overdue.

The new Duelist is a completely different design: puffer vest, patched denim, a katana across the back. Still fast and light, but grounded in the same world as everyone else.

Ability wise, Dexterous used to be a pure passive. When chosen for any encounter, you may choose to contribute 1 or 2 cards. That's it. Flexible, but it never gave you anything, it just let you spend less.

Now: **when you take part in any encounter, you may contribute 1 or 2 cards. When you contribute 2 cards, draw 1 card from the discard pile.**

So there's an actual reason to go in heavy. Committing two cards partially pays for itself. It turns a passive into a small engine, and it gives the player a real choice every encounter instead of just a permission slip.

# DEADEYE → SENTRY [HUNTER]

![](%5BBLUE%5D%20SENTRY.png)

Same face and hat, but the outfit's been reworked. More protective gear now, with gloves added, and the rifle's got a brown accent instead of pure black. The ability is now Crossfire.

Old Deadeye: twice per game, whenever an encounter is drawn, reduce the damage required by 2.

That's fine, but it's a flat discount that could be spent on literally anything, so it always got spent on whatever came up first. There was no thinking involved, and once it was gone she was a blank card for the rest of the game.

New Crossfire: **mark another player. Whenever that player takes part in an encounter, [-1] danger.**

She's one of four cards running MARK this set: place your character card onto someone else's and it stays there until, on your turn, you move it or take it back. Sentry marks whoever she's covering, and every encounter that player walks into gets quieter for it. The first draft read "whenever you and that player both take part in an encounter," but that made her ability depend on the encounter leader choosing to put her in the same fight as her target, which isn't her decision to make. Dropping "you" from the condition means she's covering that player whether or not she's standing next to them, which is what a sniper is actually for.

That's also why she went from Deadeye to Sentry, and not to Captain, which was the name on the table for a while. The old name was about the rifle. Captain was about leading a fight. Sentry is about watching someone else's, which is exactly what Crossfire does now.

# SURVIVALIST [RANGER → HUNTER]

![](%5BBLUE%5D%20SURVIVALIST.png)

Art wise he's the one who most obviously "went through something" now. Torn sleeve, bloodied bandaging up the arm, armour plate on the shoulder. The old version was just a guy in a hoodie holding a crossbow.

Changed to Blue Class. That color swap isn't cosmetic, he moved factions, from Ranger to Hunter. The original plan was to move Disruptor into Hunter instead, and every ability we wrote for her afterwards fought that placement. 

Recover used to be: whenever a zombie encounter the Survivalist takes part in **succeeds**, he's refunded a random supply card. Daybreak only.

Two problems. It only paid out on a win, and it only worked in one phase, so on a bad night he did nothing, which is the exact thing we're trying to kill.

New Recover: **whenever you take part in a zombie encounter, once the results are revealed, reshuffle the contributed supplies and draw one at random.** All Phase.

Now it triggers on every zombie encounter he joins, win or lose. Losing a fight and pulling something back out of the pile is very on-theme for a survivalist, and it means he's contributing every single round instead of only on good ones. 

# STRANGER [OUTLAW]

![](%5BBROWN%5D%20STRANGER.png)

Design is the exact same silhouette, because it was already working. Goggles, scarf, layered rags, all kept. What changed is the godray behind him: brighter now, and tapered off instead of flat, which gives the card more contrast. We also fixed the shading so he pops instead of sitting behind the godray, which used to wash the blacks into a bright grey. The pants keep their original color instead of getting flattened by that light. This fix is on every character in 4.1, but it shows the most here.

The ability change is a balance fix. Old Elude: your Secret Role **and inventory** can never be checked by any player.

That was too much. If nobody can check your role and nobody can look at your hand, there is literally no way to get information about you, and the other players just have to guess. Not fun to play against.

New Elude: **your Secret Role can never be checked by any player. Your hand of cards can still be looked through.**

So he keeps the thing that makes him the Stranger, but there's now a crack in it. The Informant can go through his hand. What he's carrying can give him away even when what he *is* can't. That gives the table something to work with.

# TRICKSTER [OUTLAW]

![](%5BBROWN%5D%20TRICKSTER.png)

The mask stays, because those pink lenses are the most recognisable thing on the card, and we made the goggles rounder so it feels like he's staring at you. Everything else got rebuilt. He used to be in a fitted vest and cargo pants, which honestly made him look like a soldier who happened to own a gas mask. Now he's in a long hooded robe with the hood up, and swapped the metal staff for a huge harpoon, giving him a bit of a grim reaper vibe. The silhouette went from tactical to something a lot more unsettling. He looks like he's about to do something to you.

Only one real change here: we cleaned up the ability text. It used to be three lines with an awkward 'this ability does not affect amnesiacs' tacked onto the end. Now it's one clean sentence: swap character cards with another player. Previous Tricksters and Amnesiacs cannot be targeted. Same rule, half the words, and the keywords are highlighted on the card so you can actually see what's protected at a glance. We're doing that highlighting across the whole set now.

# BRAWLER → WARDEN [HUNTER → OUTLAW]

![](%5BBROWN%5D%20WARDEN.png)

Same job he's always had, get in someone's way, but it took a longer path to get here than it looks. We actually did up a design for Brawler first, but that design ended up fitting the Duelist better, so it went there instead. Brawler's actual job, the lockdown ability, got handed to a new character called Sheriff. But Sheriff was still sitting in the Hunter class, and Hunter is combat, not interference, so that didn't fit. We moved him to Outlaw, which is the interference class, and renamed him Warden, since having a Sheriff sit in the Outlaw faction felt like a contradiction.

New Arrest: **mark another player. Their ability is blocked.**

He's the first of four cards running MARK this set: place your character card onto another player's and it stays there until, on your turn, you move it to someone else or take it back. For Warden that means he can hold one ability shut for as long as he's willing to sit on that player, but the moment he wants to lock someone new down, the old target opens back up. It's also the strongest card in the set for a simple reason: everyone at the table can see the card sitting there. An arrest isn't a secret, it's an argument, and that visible physical placement generates more table drama than anything else in the set.

That's the trade the mark makes for him specifically. He only gets one. Leaving it on the same person all game means he isn't watching anyone else, and moving it costs him his turn to do it.

# BLACKSMITH [RANGER]

![](%5BGREEN%5D%20BLACKSMITH.png)

The old Blacksmith was welding mask and overalls. Very literal, very village blacksmith. The new one is in a trench coat with makeshift arm armour built from belts and wraps, which reads a lot more combat-ready than someone repairing gear at a bench.

Reforge is a much better ability now. It used to be: shuffle the discard pile, draw 3 cards, take a supply card to keep or give away, and if no supply is drawn, you get nothing.

That last clause is the problem. You could use your ability and walk away with nothing at all, through no fault of your own. Nobody enjoys that.

New Reforge: **discard 2 [ARMAMENT] supply cards to take one card of your choice from the discard pile.**

Now it's a trade, not a gamble. You pay a real, specific price, two Armaments, which are cards you'd otherwise want to use, and in return you get exactly the card you need. No randomness, no dead activations, and it hooks straight into the typed supplies from the setup overhaul.

# FORAGER [RANGER]

![](%5BGREEN%5D%20FORAGER.png)

The old Forager was the clearest example of the "doesn't feel apocalyptic" problem. Blue apron, yellow bandana, wicker basket of vegetables. She looked like she was at a farmer's market.

New Forager: a worn out beanie, worn jacket, big canvas duffel, axe over the shoulder. Same job, completely different world.

Ability wise, Allocate used to be "twice per game, draw 6 cards from the discard pile and give the supplies to any player(s), return any encounter cards to the discard pile."

Long, fiddly, and it made you the table's accountant for a turn.

New Allocate: **shuffle the discard pile and draw 4 cards. Keep any supply cards you want, then place the rest into the shared inventory.**

Fewer cards, no encounter-sorting step, and the leftovers go into the shared inventory instead of you personally handing them out one by one. It's faster, it feeds the group, and the twice-per-game cap is gone so she can keep the team supplied all game.

# PROSPECTOR [OUTLAW → HUNTER]

![](%5BGREEN%5D%20PROSPECTOR.png)

Prospector's design was already one of the stronger ones, so this was mostly a repaint. Same godray fix as the rest of the set: adjusted so her colors pop instead of washing out. We also swapped the backpack for a simpler one, since the old one made her look cluttered. Better shading, cleaner detector, the pouches actually read as pouches now.

Changed to Green Class.

The ability got gutted and rebuilt though. Old Stockpile was a passive: hand limit is 6, and at the start of the game you search the deck for 2 supply cards.

That's a setup bonus. You get it before the game really begins, and then you're a normal player for the next hour. Worse, it made the deck-search a bookkeeping step during setup, which is exactly what we were trying to get away from.

New **Gold Rush:** whenever you draw a supply card, you may draw one additional card. If you draw an encounter card instead, you must face it immediately.

Now she's a push-your-luck character. Every draw is a real decision, all game long, and there's a genuine downside sitting right there in the text. Greed can walk you straight into an encounter you weren't ready for. That's a much better fit for a Ranger, and it means she's the most active she's ever been.

# OUTRUNNER → DISRUPTOR [SCRAPPER]

![](%5BOLIVE%5D%20OUTRUNNER.png)

New name, new ability, new art. This one travelled the furthest from where it started.

We took the mask off first. The old Outrunner was fully covered. Gas mask, fur hood, nothing visible. While it looked good, we already had Trickster and Stranger hiding their faces. Three masked characters out of eighteen is too many when the complaint is that the cast doesn't feel distinct.

So now she has a face, and she's holding the thing she actually uses. Two flasks, ready to throw. The old art had her standing there with her arms folded, which told you nothing about what she does on your turn.

The ability changed direction entirely, twice now. Old Evade: twice per game, when an encounter is drawn, reshuffle that card back into the encounter deck.

That's a personal undo button. It's fine, but it only ever fixes your own bad luck.

New **Decoy:** look at the top 3 encounter cards. Place 1 on the bottom of the deck and return the rest in any order.

No more Fallback Row, no more supply cost, no more phase lock. She looks at what's coming and buries the worst of it at the bottom of the deck, in full view of the group. Pathfinder can only rearrange what's coming and Surveyor can only pick which of three cards you draw, but neither of them can actually get rid of an encounter. Disruptor is the only character in the set who can make one go away for good.

That's also why the ability is Decoy now. She isn't dodging anything, she's making one of the next three encounters disappear and scattering the other two back into the pile. And it's why she's a Disruptor and not an Outrunner. The old name was about running away. The new one is about what she does to the board.

# PATHFINDER [SCRAPPER]

![](%5BOLIVE%5D%20PATHFINDER.png)

The old Pathfinder was holding a paper map in a puffy jacket with a handbag. She read like a tourist who'd got lost.

The new one has short hair, a dark coat, and a crossbow. Still the navigator, but someone who can handle what she finds out there.

Scout is mechanically the same effect, look at and rearrange the next 6 encounter cards from the top of the deck, but the limiter swapped. It used to be once per game. Now it's **discard 1 [SAMPLE] supply card**.

That's the trade we keep making across 4.1. Instead of counting uses, we charge you. Stacking the next six encounters is a genuinely powerful play, and now you can do it as often as you can pay for it, which turns Samples into something you're actually deciding what to do with.

# SURVEYOR [SCRAPPER]

![](%5BOLIVE%5D%20SURVEYOR.png)

Look wise he went from "guy in a cap with binoculars" to a proper ranger. Brimmed hat, beard, field harness. Same idea, executed a lot better.

The ability changed name and target both. Old Lookout: look at the next 3 encounter cards from the top of the deck, then place them back **in the same order**.

So you got information and nothing else. You knew what was coming and could do absolutely nothing about it.

New Overwatch: **mark another player. Whenever they draw a card, look at the top 2 cards and choose which one they draw.**

Same idea as the old Lookout, seeing ahead of a draw, but he's not stacking his own deck anymore. He's the third of four cards running MARK: place your character card onto someone else's and it stays there until you move it or take it back. Once he's watching someone, every card that player draws goes through him first. He only picks between two options instead of arranging six, but he can do it on every draw for as long as he keeps his eye on them, not once and done.

That's also why Lookout became Overwatch. The old name was about him looking at cards. The new one is about him watching a person.

# ENFORCER [DETECTIVE]

![](%5BRED%5D%20ENFORCER.png)

The Enforcer's old jacket was bright red, which fought with the red Detective card frame and made the whole card muddy. He's in a darker field jacket and slate cap now. Same pose, same character, just readable.

Investigate isn't a nomination trigger anymore, it's a mark. Old Investigate: after an unsuccessful escape nomination, you may view the Secret Role of a nominated player on your next turn.

That only fired when a nomination failed, so on a quiet game he could go rounds without a single lead.

New Investigate: **mark another player. Whenever they join an encounter, look at their hand before they contribute.**

He's the fourth of four cards running MARK, and it's the one that plays best against how encounters actually work: contributions go in face down and only get revealed once the fight resolves. Enforcer already saw the hand before those cards went in. Everyone else at the table only learns what someone had after the fact, he's the one who knew going in, so he's the one who can call it when a player claims that's all they had.

And of course, no more once per game. Once he's got someone marked, every encounter they join is another look.

# TRACER → INFORMANT [DETECTIVE]

![](%5BRED%5D%20INFORMANT.png)

Renamed, kept the ability name Intercept, and it's probably the cleanest illustration of the whole 4.1 philosophy.

Old Tracer: once per game, take and examine **all hand cards of 2 players** of your choice.

Huge effect, exactly once. You'd sit on it for half the game waiting for the perfect moment, use it, and then be a spectator.

New Informant: **discard 1 card of your choice. Choose one player, then take and look through their entire hand of cards. You may target the same player again on future turns.**

Half the reach, one player instead of two, but you can do it every turn you're willing to pay for it, and you can go back to the same person if you think they picked something up. She went from one enormous moment to being a constant, ongoing presence, and the discard cost means it's never free.

# INSPECTOR [DETECTIVE]

![](%5BRED%5D%20INSPECTOR.png)

The old Inspector was a cap, a scarf and a hatchet. He looked like anyone else in the woods. The new one is in a peaked officer's cap and full uniform with a chest rig. He looks like someone who can actually tell two other people what to do, which matters because that's literally his ability.

Delegate is unchanged in effect: **select 2 other players and designate a player to check the Secret Role of the other.** We added "other" to make it explicit that you can't include yourself, and removed the once-per-game line.

He's the strangest Detective in the set, because he never learns anything himself. He hands the information to someone else and then has to watch what they do with it. Making that repeatable is what makes him work. Once per game it was a novelty. Every Nightfall, it's a whole style of play.

# So, what does all this actually change?

Removing the limits was never just about tidying up card text. It changes how the game plays. You're not spending your one ability, then coasting to the finish line. Every turn now has something you can actually do, if you're willing to pay for it.

That also speeds the whole game up, and it makes it harder for Carriers to hide. With this many reusable abilities running at once, information keeps surfacing whether anyone likes it or not. But the answer isn't panic, it's deduction. Take the Geneticist. If she's out there repeatedly asking players to reveal, and revealing her own role back to them, that itself is a tell. A player with something to lose doesn't offer to show their hand first. Logic like that is now half the game.

The Virologist plays into the same idea, but through cost instead of behavior. Diagnose now costs a Sample supply card, not Medical. Samples are the one thing every escape plan actually needs, so every time she checks someone, she's burning a resource the whole table is short on. That's a decision with weight behind it, not a free action.

And this is where the Detective faction earns its place. Scientists and the Outlander deal in facts. What they learn is confirmed, no ambiguity. Detectives don't get that. What they get is Indirect Intel, a hand seen, a pattern noticed, a player watched over several turns. It might point at something. It might mean nothing. It's suspicion, not proof, and that's the point. Not every kind of knowing in this game should feel the same.
